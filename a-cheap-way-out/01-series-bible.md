# A Cheap Way Out — Series Bible (Book 1)

## 1. The World

**The Republic of Aumery.** A democratic republic with an elected Assembly, a free press that is mostly owned by someone, and courts that work well for anyone who can afford them. The culture should feel faintly familiar: crowded commuter trains, tea stalls, extended families sharing one flat, exam-season panic, wedding debt. All the vocabulary is invented.

**Esterhal** is the capital and the money. **Coldwell** is a dying outskirts town ninety minutes away by slow train. It was built on rime mining, and the mine is now mostly exhausted. The town has a closed pit, a row of abandoned cold-stores, a canal, and too many pawn shops. Arc 1 takes place in Coldwell.

**Currency: the marl.** Reference prices:
- A cup of tea at a stall: 9 marl.
- A Hopline delivery drop pays the rider 38 marl before deductions.
- A one-room flat in Coldwell: 6,200 marl a month.
- Starting salary at Rung One for a placed kindled graduate: 55,000 marl a month.
- Street price of natural rime: roughly 300 marl a gram, or 300,000 a kilo.

## 2. The Substance: Rime

**Natural rime** is a pale blue crystalline mineral found in cold, deep salt deposits. It sublimates if it is kept warm. Supply is finite, the known deposits are nearly gone, and every gram is licensed through the **Assay Bureau**. Scarcity is the whole economy.

Rime does different things to different bodies:
- **Kindled** people use it as a sharpener. A maintenance dose extends an ability's range, duration or precision. Placed kindled get it on prescription through their employer. Unplaced kindled buy it on the street at street prices.
- **Dry** people take it as a narcotic: cold, clean euphoria, then a long grey crash. It is moderately addictive, and the crash is where the damage happens.
- The mining companies used to dose Coldwell's miners so they could work the cold seams. That created a generation of systemic addicts, and then the companies left.

**Synthetic rime** (official name: *Lantern*; later street name: *glass*) does not officially exist. In Arc 1 it appears only as a single prototype vial. Its effects, side effects and full mystery belong to Arcs 2 and 3. What Arc 1 establishes:
- Abilities near it go **blank** (see §4).
- It carries a joint Assay Bureau and Meridane Group lot number. The state and the market made it together.

## 3. Kindling, Placement, and Class

**Kindled** people have the rare biology that rime can awaken. That's about 1 in 400 people. A **marrow panel** at age 16 screens for it. Awakening is done under licence at an **Institute**. Unlicensed awakening is a crime, though a common one.

**The Assay Bureau** classifies every kindled person with:
- a **class code** (for example *PC-H*: precognition, hunch-class);
- an **instability rating** from 1 to 5;
- a **placement value** estimate.

**Underwriting.** Every House must insure the kindled people it employs. Insurers price risk from the instability rating. A rating of 4 or 5 means *uninsurable*, so no House will hire you, however strong the ability is. This is the real gatekeeper.

**The Drive.** This is Placement Week at an Institute. Houses set up recruitment tables. Students sit tests and interviews and accept a **bond**: a 3–5 year contract. Breaking it costs a **release fee**, usually two years' salary, which most families borrow to pay.

**Rungs.** Within a House, employees promote through fixed Rungs One to Six on an **appraisal cycle**. Appraisal scores **utility to the business**, not raw power. A girl who can keep cut flowers fresh for a month is Rung Five at a cosmetics House. A man who can knock down a wall is Rung Two at a demolition firm, because his insurance costs more than he earns them.

**Houses** are corporate groups organised by sector: vanity houses (cosmetics), tech, defense, consumer electronics, health. The ones that matter in Book 1:
- **Meridane Group**: health and cosmetics. Its chair sits on the Roll.
- **Kessard Defense**: mentioned, Arc 2.

**The Roll** is about a hundred families, 104 on the last count anyone admits to, who hold most of the Republic's wealth. Nobody is on the Roll because of their power. They got there through state contracts, licensing monopolies on rime, Assembly members who owe them, and tax treatment written by their own lawyers. Their **civic foundations** fund the campaigns of the politicians who regulate them.

**Class without hierarchy.** There are no titles. Class shows up as: whose rime is prescribed and whose is bought; who gets a civic card without a grey stamp; who rides first carriage; who can afford a release fee; who gets questioned and who gets a lawyer.

**The grey stamp.** A juvenile or criminal record leaves a grey stamp on your **civic card**. It bars you from Institutes and from formal employment above "casual tier". It also charges you **supervision fees**, which the state bills to you for the privilege of being supervised.

**The State.** The Assembly and ministers are elected. The local police is **the Watch**. Coldwell's Assembly member is **Imre Dask**, who is campaigning on *Coldwell Renewal*: selling the old mine site to a Meridane subsidiary for a data centre.

## 4. The Power: Butterfly-Effect Foresight

**What he believes it is (Bureau classification):** *PC-H, precognition, hunch-class. Short-range, unreliable. Instability 4. Adverse episodes noted. Placement value: nil.* He thinks he gets bad hunches about what's about to happen.

**What it actually is:** he reads **ripples**. When he holds a *small action of his own* in mind (tip a cup, step left, say one word, let a door swing), he perceives the chain of consequences that action would start. He isn't seeing the future. He's seeing what he would *cause*. Arc 1 ends with him understanding this partially. Arc 2 deals with the full rules and the source of the instability.

**Rules**
1. **Trigger.** The candidate action must be small, physical or verbal, and his own. Early on, readings fire *involuntarily* when he hesitates over a small act under stress. From Chapter 5 he learns to trigger them deliberately by putting a hand on the object or forming the word.
2. **Shape.** The first two or three links of the cascade are sharp. Later links blur.
3. **Stones.** Other people's free decisions are *stones* in the stream. They can bend a cascade after he has read it. The more people involved, the less reliable the far end.
4. **Horizon.** Normally 30 seconds to about 15 minutes, and it varies from day to day. This is the instability: he can't promise a client how far he'll see tomorrow. Rime extends the horizon to about an hour but makes the stones shift more.
5. **Range.** Only things he can currently perceive with his senses.
6. **The blank.** Any cascade that passes within about two metres of the Lantern vial goes white. He can't read through it. (See §6.)

**Cost: Commitment.** Once he has read a ripple, he has to *begin* that action within **the window**: about a count of sixty. Beginning is enough. Finishing isn't required. If he doesn't begin, the ripple **snaps back**:
- he gets a nosebleed and a white-out of his vision lasting 5–20 seconds, depending on how big the abandoned ripple was;
- something near him goes wrong in a way connected to the action he skipped. The energy has to land somewhere, and it lands close.

Readings stack. If he reads two options, he owes both. So he can't browse his options, and every reading is a purchase. In a fight, reading too much leaves him with a pile of commitments that contradict each other. The Bureau logged his early snap-backs as "adverse episodes", and those logs are part of why he was rated Instability 4.

**Flaw: the Forced Bet.**
- **Trigger:** someone offers him an explicit wager with a stake, and at least two other people can hear it.
- **Rule:** he must accept, and he must pay if he loses. If he refuses, dodges or welshes, his ability shuts off for **one hour** (the *dark hour*).
- It doesn't force him to *offer* bets, and offering them is how he exploits it.
- Nobody knows about it at the start of the story. He learned about it in the Reform, and it cost him two teeth.
- Budget for Arc 1: **two trouble triggers** (Ch 9 and Ch 24), **one exploit** (Ch 20), and **one low-stakes demonstration** (Ch 3).

## 5. Ability Roster (Arc 1)

Every cost directly counters its ability.

| Who | Ability | Cost / counter |
|---|---|---|
| Tully Brask | **Brand**: his palms heat to iron-searing temperatures on contact | His palms go numb for hours afterwards, so he can't feel his grip and drops things |
| Pia "Saint" Moranne | **Second Hand**: after watching anyone write for ten seconds, she can forge their hand flawlessly | Each forgery overwrites her own signature, which drifts toward her last mark. Her own ID, bank and contracts get flagged. She relearns her name from an old school notebook |
| Wen Castry | **Echo**: she can listen for any sound she has heard before (a voice, an engine) up to about a kilometre away | While she listens she is completely deaf to everything near her |
| Ludo Farrance | **Ballast**: anything he holds becomes up to ten times heavier | His own body becomes lighter by the same factor while the ability is active. A heavy punch throws him backwards, and a shove sends him flying |
| Cobb Ainsel | **Tumbler**: he hears the pins inside locks | Hypersensitive hearing while the ability is active. A loud noise can drop him |
| Sefton Glade | **Keep**: he knows the direction and distance of anything he has touched with bare skin, until he next sleeps. Maximum seven at once | The pull runs both ways. Anything he keeps leans toward him: coins drift, and people feel an itch to turn toward him. His prey can use it to find *him*. Insomniac, because sleeping wipes his keeps |
| Neve Ashcombe (Meridane recruiter) | **Shelf-life**: she can tell to the day when anything organic will spoil | It works on people too. She can't stop knowing |

## 6. The Central Mystery (seeded in Arc 1, kept open)

- The Lantern vial, **Lot 0**, is stamped with both an Assay Bureau custody seal and a Meridane serial. Its label reads: *NOT FOR AWAKENING TRIALS*.
- It **blanks abilities** within about two metres. Oren's ripples go white, and Glade's Keep can't track it. Why it does this is the mystery.
- Hiding a pinned person or object near the vial makes Glade's trace vanish. The vanishing is itself a signal. (Ch 17)
- Arc 1 never answers what Lantern does if someone takes it, why it was being moved through Coldwell, or who ordered Glade. Arc 1 does reveal that Oren *causes* rather than *foresees*, and that the Bureau knows more about his classification than it put on paper.

## 7. Characters

**Oren Kalt**, 19. Nickname **"Cheap"**, from the Reform, because his Bureau certificate says *placement value: nil*. First person, past tense.
- *Situation:* three months out of the Coldwell Juvenile Reform. He did three years for driving a van for his uncle's crew. The crew walked and he didn't. He owes **41,300 marl** in supervision fees, garnished at 30% of his wages. He rides for Hopline on a borrowed bike, with a rider rating of 4.1 that is falling. He lives with his grandmother.
- *Wants now:* to make rent, clear the fees and stay invisible. He'd settle for a cheap way out.
- *Voice:* he thinks in prices, odds and costs. He's deadpan, and most of his jokes stay in his head rather than out loud. His lies are short and plain. He never names his feelings; they show in what he does (he fixes Nan's tap at 3 a.m.). He is petty about small injustices and numb about big ones. He's a good observer of other people's tells and a poor observer of his own.
- *Cunning:* he uses whichever method fits the moment: a lie, setting someone else up, or presenting his "unstable" rating as harmless.
- *Mistakes:* he over-reads under pressure and gets buried in commitments. He trusts his own read of people too much, especially Ludo. He also underestimates dry people like Mott.

**Nan (Ilse Kalt)**, 71. Oren's grandmother. She runs a pickle-and-preserves stall, plays the state lottery religiously, and knows everybody. She is not sweet. She's funny, mercenary and unshockable. She never asks where money comes from, and she counts it twice.

**The crew: the Yard.** Hesk's people, working out of a scrapyard beside the old cold-stores. They rob rime shipments, licensed dispensaries and assay vans.
- **Hesk Arrund**, 58. Leader. Former mine foreman. A systemic rime addict: the company dosed him and then left. He is sentimental, careful and capable of terrible things in a quiet voice. He wants his nephew back and one last big score before his lungs give out. Voice: proverbs he gets slightly wrong, long pauses, and he calls everyone "son", including the women.
- **Tully Brask**, 26. Hesk's nephew, kindled (Brand). Oren kills him in Chapter 1 by accident. Tully had ordered the food to *recruit* Oren as a driver, not to rob him.
- **Ludo Farrance**, 34. Hesk's lieutenant, kindled (Ballast). He's ambitious and charming in short bursts. A cruel user of the drug: he cuts rime with chalk and floor cleaner and sells it to addicts he despises. He has been quietly draining the missing Tully's bank account. He wants Hesk's seat. Voice: nicknames, bets and constant needling.
- **Pia "Saint" Moranne**, 31. Forger and bookkeeper, kindled (Second Hand). An Institute dropout who owes a release fee on a bond she broke. She wants to get clear of the debt and the Yard, and she will betray anyone politely. Voice: precise and dry. She corrects grammar during robberies.
- **Bram Uteki**, 29. Muscle, dry. Former regional wrestler, gentle, always eating. He wants to open a noodle-and-skewer stall, and he has a name picked out. Voice: few words, kind, literal. He dies in Chapter 26.
- **Wen Castry**, 17. Lookout and second driver, kindled (Echo), unlicensed awakening. She talks fast and wants an adult cut. Her mother is a rime addict, which Wen treats as a logistics problem and not a tragedy. She witnessed part of Chapter 1.
- **Cobb Ainsel**, 63. Safecracker, kindled (Tumbler). Old, polite, deaf in one ear on purpose. Killed by Glade in Chapter 14.

**The law**
- **Sergeant Hal Mott**, 52. The Watch, Coldwell. Dry. He's on Hesk's payroll and honest about everything else. He's patient, eats boiled sweets and remembers everything. He is the most dangerous person in the book who has no power. He wants a quiet pension, and he wants to know everything in his town. By the end he owns a piece of Oren.
- **Ms. Dace Ollery**, 40s. Oren's supervision officer. A bureaucrat who isn't cruel, just tired. She works off a terminal that is always loading.

**The antagonist**
- **Sefton Glade**, mid-40s. A contractor for Meridane, kindled (Keep). Courteous, tidy, an insomniac. He keeps an itemised expense log that includes the people he kills ("Dispatcher, Hopline, 1 hr, incidentals: towel"). He appraises everyone: "What band would they have given you? Be honest." He sees people purely as market values, and that's what makes him frightening rather than any rage. He eats salted plum seeds and spits the stones into his palm.
- He is not introduced until Chapter 10. He hunts by following a paper trail and leaving bodies behind him, because the vial blanks his ability.

**The structure above them** (mostly offstage in Arc 1)
- **Meridane Group**: its chair, Ottilie Varn, is on the Roll.
- **Imre Dask**: Assembly member for Coldwell, funded by Meridane's civic foundation.
- **The Assay Bureau**: co-owner of Lantern Lot 0.

## 8. Glossary

- **Appraisal**: the six-monthly review that decides promotion between Rungs.
- **Assay Bureau**: the state agency that licenses rime and classifies the kindled.
- **Blank**: the dead zone around Lantern in which abilities can't read.
- **Bond**: a bonded placement contract, 3–5 years.
- **Civic card**: the national ID.
- **Cold-stores**: abandoned refrigerated vaults from the mining era.
- **Dark hour**: the hour Oren's ability is off after he refuses a bet.
- **Drive, the**: Placement Week at an Institute.
- **Dry**: not kindled.
- **Glass**: the future street name for synthetic rime.
- **Grey stamp**: the record mark on a civic card.
- **Hopline**: the delivery dispatch company.
- **House**: a corporate group.
- **Keep**: Glade's tracking ability. A *pin* is one tracked thing.
- **Kindled**: awakened, powered.
- **Lantern**: the official name of synthetic rime.
- **Marl**: the currency.
- **Marrow panel**: the age-16 screening for kindled biology.
- **Reform, the**: juvenile detention.
- **Release fee**: the cost of breaking a bond.
- **Rime**: the natural substance.
- **Ripple**: one of Oren's readings.
- **Roll, the**: the ~100 families who hold most of the wealth.
- **Rung**: a pay and promotion tier within a House.
- **Snap-back**: the penalty for not acting on a ripple.
- **Stones**: other people's free choices inside a ripple.
- **Underwriting**: insuring kindled employees. *Uninsurable* means unemployable.
- **Watch, the**: the local police.
- **Window**: the roughly sixty-second commitment period after a ripple.
- **Yard, the**: Hesk's crew and its scrapyard base.
